# 32 Principles of a Viral Product

Distilled from **Marc Lou ([@marclou](https://x.com/marclou))** — indie hacker and founder of
multiple viral products. These 32 principles cover everything from copy to pricing to product strategy.

> **Last updated:** June 12, 2025
> **Source:** [@marclou thread](https://x.com/marclou) — "32 Principles of a Viral Product"

## Cluster 1 — Copy & Messaging

### P7 — Headline a fifth grader can understand
Complexity kills curiosity. Use simple words. Your mum should get it.
**For agent-finance:** "See where your money goes" beats "AI-powered personal finance analytics platform."

### P3 — Numbers instead of adjectives
"Fast" is forgettable. "Save 4 hours every week" isn't.
**The move:** Audit every adjective on the page. Replace with a specific number, time, or outcome.
**For agent-finance:** "Never miss a subscription charge again" → "Catch hidden subscriptions in under 60 seconds."

### P18 — Emotional headline
People don't remember features. They remember feelings. Your headline should make people laugh, say wow, or think "what the f*** is this."
**The move:** Write for humans. Test: after reading your headline, does the reader *feel* something?

### P17 — Headline people remember the next day
Write five headlines. Show them to friends. Wait 24 hours and ask which one they remember.
**The move:** Keep the one that sticks. Discard the clever ones nobody recalls.

### P26 — No weak words
"most", "many", "rarely" weaken your message because nobody knows what they mean.
**The move:** Replace every hedge with a clear claim: not "many users save money" → "Users find an average of R$312 in unused subscriptions."
**Banned words for agent-finance copy:** most, many, some, often, usually, various, several, robust, comprehensive, powerful.

### P9 — Copy only you could write
If a competitor could copy-paste your landing page onto their website, your copy is too generic.
**The move:** Write from the founder's own experience. Include a specific story, a specific number, a specific frustration that only you lived through.

### P14 — Steal best copy from customers
Customers already describe your product better than you do. Write like your customers talk.
**The move:** Pull exact phrases from support tickets, app store reviews, DMs, and user interviews. Paste them verbatim into headlines and body copy.

### P24 — Sell a human desire, not a feature
People buy more money, more time, better health, more status, or less pain. Features are just vehicles.
**For agent-finance:** Don't sell "transaction categorization." Sell "finally knowing where your money went" (less mental load) or "stop feeling guilty every time you check your bank app" (less pain).

## Cluster 2 — Landing Page Structure

### P20 — Hero section sells the whole product
80% of visitors won't scroll past the hero. If they don't understand the product and want it within a few seconds, you've already lost. Fix the hero first.
**Checklist for agent-finance hero:**
- [ ] What the product is (1 sentence, fifth-grade level)
- [ ] Who it's for (implicit or explicit)
- [ ] One CTA (not two)
- [ ] Visual proof (screenshot, demo, or number)

### P6 — One idea per screen
Don't try to say everything at once. One screen should communicate one idea and nothing else.
**The move:** Print each screen section. Can you summarize it in 5 words? If not, cut.

### P10 — Show the product before explaining it
A demo communicates more than paragraphs of text. Show. Don't tell.
**For agent-finance:** An animated GIF of the dashboard loading real data beats four bullet points about features.

### P4 — Footer people want to share
97% of visitors won't buy, but they might share. People remember what they see last.
**The move:** End with something memorable — a bold statement, a surprising stat, a single powerful sentence about what the product stands for.

### P21 — Show empathy before selling
Before people trust your solution, they need to believe you understand their problem.
**The move:** Describe the problem better than the customer can. Mirror their frustration back at them before introducing your product.

## Cluster 3 — Pricing & Monetization

### P12 — Popcorn Pricing
Your visitors came to buy a product, not study a spreadsheet. Every pricing tier you add creates another decision and another reason to leave.
**The move:** Three choices only: Good. Better. Best. No more.

### P16 — Make pricing impossible to miss
The pricing section is one of the first places visitors look. They use it to understand the product, not just the price.
**The move:** Put "Pricing" in the navigation header. Never hide it.

### P27 — Avoid subscriptions if possible
People already pay for enough subscriptions. One-time payments are 10× easier to sell.
**For agent-finance:** If recurring billing is necessary (for AI/API costs), frame it differently: "Lifetime access for early users" or a one-time setup + low monthly AI cost.

### P32 — Be more expensive than competitors
Nobody talks about the second cheapest option. Charge more.
**The move:** Higher price = higher perceived value. If you're cheap, you attract cost-sensitive users with high churn and low LTV.

### P8 — Hard paywall
Signups don't pay the bills. If nobody is willing to pull out their credit card, you don't have validation. Ask for payment before asking for data.

### P1 — No free plan (or kill it)
Free users are leeches. Less than 3% of free users ever convert. They increase support, server costs, and make you build features your paying customers don't want.
**Exception:** Only keep a free tier if it's a pure acquisition channel that generates viral loops (referrals, embeds, social sharing). Otherwise, remove it.

## Cluster 4 — Product Strategy

### P11 — Do one thing
The more things you do, the less people remember. People don't remember Swiss Army knives. They remember the tool that solved their problem.
**For agent-finance:** Pick one superpower. Examples: "The app that finds hidden subscriptions." "The finance app that shows you where your money actually went." Be known for that one thing.

### P13 — Ride a wave
Build around trends, technologies, and problems people are already discussing. The wave does half the marketing for you.
**For agent-finance:** Current waves to ride: AI agents, open banking, financial anxiety (post-inflation), gig economy income tracking.

### P19 — Do something people have never seen before
Nobody shares another clone. Surprise people.
**The move:** Find one mechanic, visual, or feature that is genuinely novel. Design for the screenshot and the "have you seen this?" DM.

### P23 — Name people remember
Use words people already know. Avoid wordplay, made-up words, and names that require explanation.

## Cluster 5 — Social Proof & Trust

### P29 — No launch without testimonials
A landing page without testimonials is asking strangers to trust you blindly.
**The move:** Get 3–5 users (beta testers, friends, early customers) before writing a single line of ad copy. Collect their exact words. Use verbatim quotes with real names and photos.

### P31 — Compare to competitors
People don't care what your product does. They care why they should switch.
**The move:** Build a simple comparison table. Features your customers care about. Make the decision obvious.

### P15 — Show the founder's face
People buy from people. A screen recording from the founder beats a corporate promo video.
**For agent-finance:** Record a 60-second Loom of the founder using the product. Embed it in the hero or the "About" section.

## Cluster 6 — CTAs & UX

### P22 — One call to action
Every extra button creates hesitation. When people have multiple paths, many choose none.
**The move:** One CTA per page section. One CTA in the nav. Same destination — remove all other links.

### P28 — CTA that says what happens next
"Get Started" means nothing. "Analyze My Finances" tells people exactly what they're about to do.
**For agent-finance CTA options:**
- "Connect My Bank Account" ✅
- "See My Spending Breakdown" ✅
- "Find My Hidden Subscriptions" ✅
- "Get Started" ❌
- "Sign Up Free" ❌

### P25 — Let people try before buying
Don't hide your best features behind a paywall. Put them on the landing page.
**The move:** Build an interactive demo, a sample dashboard with fake data, or a free tool (e.g., "Paste your bank statement, see what the AI finds") that shows real value before the purchase.

## Cluster 7 — Distribution & Shareability

### P5 — Treat OG image as a YouTube thumbnail
"If they don't click, they don't watch." Your OG image is often seen more than your actual website.
**The move:** Design the OG image with a bold claim, a number, and a visual that works even at Twitter card size. Test it at 280×150px.

### P30 — Describe in under 10 words
If you can't explain your product in one sentence, your users won't either.
**For agent-finance:** Draft five 10-word descriptions. Pick the sharpest one. That's your product's elevator pitch and Twitter bio.

### P2 — Three colors only
Every color fights for attention. The more colors you add, the less people notice what matters.
**Rule:** Black text. White (or near-white) background. One accent color for the primary CTA. That's it.
