# Onboarding & Activation

> Curated, distilled wisdom from @richardrx ("Richard — Design for startups"). Each entry is a reusable principle linked to its source post.

## An onboarding video welcomes — it doesn't teach
**Principle.** A good onboarding video isn't a manual. It welcomes, builds connection, shows the product at a glance, and points to where value comes fastest.
**Apply when.** Designing first-run onboarding or a welcome video.
**The move.** Aim at cutting TTV, support tickets, and the lost feeling — not at educating. Length follows your ICP's urgency. No actor or fancy set needed.
**Source.** [@richardrx · 2026-06-19](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2067987722954735812)

## Rising MRR with rising churn means you lost them on day one
**Principle.** When MRR and churn climb together, the user didn't leave in month 2 — they were lost the first day, dropped into a dead empty-state dashboard with nothing guiding them to value.
**Apply when.** Churn is creeping up and you're tempted to blame the product or add features.
**The move.** It isn't a feature gap — measure **TTV** (time-to-value) and get obsessed with shrinking it. With weak retention, acquiring more just fills a leaky bucket faster.
**Source.** [@richardrx · 2026-06-18](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2067591574138052804)

## Measure activation, not signups
**Principle.** Signups, session time, and tour completion all flatter you without proving the user reached value.
**Apply when.** Judging onboarding by signups or "completed the tour."
**The move.** Swap each vanity metric: signups → activation rate, session time → time-to-first-useful-action, onboarding completion → D7 retention. Find your aha moment empirically: look at what every paying customer did in week one that churned users didn't.
**Evidence.** Userpilot benchmark (547 companies): avg TTV 1d 12h 23m; top performers under 5 min. SaaS activation avg 30–37%, top quartile 40%+, under 20% = structural problem.
**Source.** [@richardrx · 2026-05-27](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2059616501544468624)

## Put friction in the right place, not zero friction everywhere
**Principle.** Friction in the wrong place kills the product; friction in the right place qualifies and retains. "Less friction" is not a universal law.
**Apply when.** Reflexively cutting clicks and fields everywhere.
**The move.** Remove friction at trial signup. Add calibrated friction: (1) trial with card filters commercial intent; (2) mandatory onboarding turns users into power-users faster; (3) enterprise demo form qualifies leads. Mechanism: effort justification (Aronson & Mills, 1959).
**Evidence.** ChartMogul 2026: opt-in trial (no card) converts 8.9%; opt-out (with card) converts 31.4%.
**Source.** [@richardrx · 2026-05-21](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2057436163841941980)

## Never ship a blank dashboard
**Principle.** The empty dashboard arrives at the user's peak of curiosity and answers it with a void — this is where most SaaS loses the trial.
**Apply when.** A new user lands post-signup on a screen with no data and no direction.
**The move.** Four fixes: (1) empty state with a next-action hint; (2) seed sample data so they see the destination; (3) one single clear action, not eight; (4) visible progress from the first click — start the bar at 20%, not 0%.
**Source.** [@richardrx · 2026-05-12](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2054283657934758021)

## Design onboarding as a behavioral trigger, not a feature tour
**Principle.** Silent non-activation — users who sign up, vanish in five minutes, and never formally churn — is an activation problem, not a product one.
**Apply when.** New users evaporate without complaint.
**The move.** Find the single behavior that statistically separates retained from lost users, make it your activation north star. Exploit the Zeigarnik effect: open small loops (complete profile, invite colleagues, send first message) so the user carries an unfinished task.
**Evidence.** Slack: teams exchanging 2,000 messages had 93% probability of staying. Facebook: 7 friends in 10 days — the company's single focus.
**Source.** [@richardrx · 2026-05-11](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2053878928494690414)

## Map the journey from session replays, not from your diagram
**Principle.** The founder's 12-step journey is built top-down (what the product wants); the user runs 4 steps bottom-up (the specific problem they opened the tab to solve).
**Apply when.** You "know" the happy path but can't state what % of users actually execute it.
**The move.** Three steps: (1) write your version of the user journey; (2) open five real session recordings from the first 7 days; (3) lay both lists side by side. Each divergence is a hypothesis to confirm or kill.
**Source.** [@richardrx · 2026-05-08](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2052711138572263474)

## Add declarative friction, cut administrative friction
**Principle.** There are two frictions: administrative (collects data the system uses later, buys the user nothing) and declarative (forces the user to state what they came to do — costs a beat, buys commitment).
**Apply when.** Auditing onboarding steps; deciding what to cut versus expand.
**The move.** Test each step: collecting only → candidate to cut. Declaring intent → candidate to expand. A declaration creates a micro-commitment to the outcome before the user touches the product.
**Evidence.** Brazilian payments platform cut time-from-signup-to-first-sale from 24.2 to 2.5 days by adding a ~30-second intent step.
**Source.** [@richardrx · 2026-05-07](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2052350703541039324)
