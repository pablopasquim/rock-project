# Metrics, Experimentation & Business Math

> Curated, distilled wisdom from @richardrx ("Richard — Design for startups"). Each entry is a reusable principle linked to its source post.

## Don't mistake signups for traction
**Principle.** Signups are the cheapest action a user takes, so they measure curiosity, not value — especially in freemium.
**Apply when.** A rising signup curve on the dashboard feels like proof of traction.
**The move.** Treat signups as top-of-funnel only. Track who returns on day 2 and day 7 (D1/D7 retention) and how many complete the value-delivering action (activation). Paid media can inflate signups while real retention stays flat.
**Source.** [@richardrx · 2026-06-11](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2065082771987394651)

## Don't bet your product on an underpowered A/B test
**Principle.** Most A/B tests in small SaaS lack the volume to prove anything, yet founders swap the whole product on the result.
**Apply when.** In traction or survival stage, ran a test for a week, saw "variant B won by 12%", and want to ship it everywhere.
**The move.** Before running, compute the minimum sample size; if you can't hit that floor in reasonable time, don't start. Test big things (headline, offer, pricing structure, onboarding) since large effects need less sample. With no volume, decide by qualitative research — five good interviews beat an underpowered A/B test.
**Source.** [@richardrx — Design for startups]

## The key metrics for a finance product
**Apply specifically to agent-finance.** These are the metrics that matter most:
- **Activation rate**: % of signups who connect their first account or enter their first transaction within 24h
- **D7 retention**: % of activated users who return in week 1
- **TTV (Time to Value)**: time from signup to first insight/aha moment (e.g., seeing their first cash flow chart)
- **NRR (Net Revenue Retention)**: are existing paying users expanding or contracting?
- **Feature adoption depth**: which dashboard widgets/reports are used daily vs. abandoned
