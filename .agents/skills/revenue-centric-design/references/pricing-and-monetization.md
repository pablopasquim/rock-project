# Pricing & Monetization Psychology

> Curated, distilled wisdom from @richardrx ("Richard — Design for startups"). Each entry is a reusable principle linked to its source post.

## The freemium trap, in numbers: higher conversion, far lower cash
**Principle.** A free plan lifts signup conversion but can crush the economics — the higher top-of-funnel number hides a worse business.
**Apply when.** Tempted by freemium's better conversion rate.
**The move.** Run the full funnel math, not just conversion rates. Freemium only pays off if free brings *organic/viral* users you didn't pay for. With AI-token costs, every free user costs real money.
**Evidence.** Same R$80k/mo traffic: freemium → 32 payers, R$6,368 MRR, real CAC ≈ R$2,540/payer. Without freemium → 300 payers, R$59,700 MRR (~10×), CAC ~R$275.
**Source.** [@richardrx · 2026-07-01](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2072312844784152628)

## Whether freemium works is decided by the cost to serve a free user
**Principle.** Freemium isn't good or bad in the abstract — the *cost of free* decides.
**Apply when.** Considering a free plan, especially as a bootstrapped founder.
**The move.** If serving a free user costs almost nothing and TTV is short, free becomes an acquisition channel (Slack). If the product runs on AI (dollar-priced tokens) or activation is long, a free account is an expensive bet. On average, only ~3–4% of freemium converts.
**Source.** [@richardrx · 2026-06-30](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2071962778072469560)

## Price is the cheapest money — stop anchoring it to the cheapest competitor
**Principle.** Pricing is a SaaS's biggest lever, yet ~90% of products are underpriced — the founder anchors on the cheapest competitor instead of on value delivered.
**Apply when.** Setting or revisiting price; fearing a "no."
**The move.** Raise toward value. A 30% price increase doesn't yield 30% MRR (some churn), but what remains is nearly pure cash — no acquisition in between. Low price costs you: less budget to reach your ICP, higher churn (cheap attracts uncommitted buyers).
**Source.** [@richardrx · 2026-06-29](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2071634185228329219)

## Make the middle plan the one you actually want to sell
**Principle.** Each plan has a behavioral job. The plan you most want to sell should sit in the middle, flanked by a decoy below and an anchor above.
**Apply when.** Building or auditing a SaaS pricing page.
**The move.** Use the decoy effect: place your target (e.g. Pro) in the middle; make the tier below it clearly inferior on one important attribute. Keep exactly three plans — four+ triggers the paradox of choice. Add a top tier (Enterprise) purely to anchor price perception.
**Evidence.** Ariely's MIT test: with the print-only decoy, 16%/84% chose online/combo; removing it flipped choices to 68%/32%, cutting combo revenue by more than half.
**Source.** [@richardrx · 2026-05-28](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2059951433827426437)

## Ask for the card in trial — but optimize for the right ICP, not raw conversion
**Principle.** Requiring a credit card multiplies trial-to-paid conversion but shrinks signups; the goal is the model that attracts and retains the right ICP.
**Apply when.** Choosing trial-with-card vs trial-without-card (or freemium).
**The move.** Run the full funnel math both ways. Trial-with-card converts harder but starves you of volume; trial-without-card floods the funnel with low-intent users.
**Evidence.** ChartMogul 2026 (US, 200 products): trial-with-card converts ~31.4% vs 8.9% without — 3×+.
**Source.** [@richardrx · 2026-05-15](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2055247161349054950)

## Frame the upgrade as a loss at the moment of value, not a feature you're selling
**Principle.** Low upgrade rates are usually a framing-and-timing problem, not a price problem.
**Apply when.** Users hit the paywall and don't upgrade, or upgrade prompts appear at the wrong moment.
**The move.** Time the upsell to the exact moment the user feels the limit — not on day 1, not in an email. Frame it as loss: "You're about to lose this transaction history" activates loss aversion far more than "Upgrade to Pro for more features."
**Source.** [@richardrx — Design for startups]
