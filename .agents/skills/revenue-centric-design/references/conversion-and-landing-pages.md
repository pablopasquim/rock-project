# Conversion & Landing Pages

> Curated, distilled wisdom from @richardrx ("Richard — Design for startups"), translated from Portuguese. Each entry is a reusable principle linked to its source post.

## "Paint the button" is judging the last line of a long cascade
**Principle.** The visual layer of a landing page is the tip of a cascade that begins at the ICP — so "make it more colorful" critiques the *end* of a process as if it were the start.
**Apply when.** Gathering or acting on LP feedback, or reaching for button color first.
**The move.** Work the cascade in order: ICP → the buyer's **awareness level** (Eugene Schwartz 5 stages) → what you say and how → visual positioning (tone → form, color, type, space). Low awareness: open on the problem, name the pain in the first fold, then the mechanism and your fix. Higher awareness: go straight to your advantages vs. alternatives.
**Source.** [@richardrx · 2026-06-23](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2069469303464730988)

## Scaling cold traffic is an honesty test for your page
**Principle.** Conversion measured on warm audiences (existing followers) is inflated — only cold traffic reveals how much of your rate is the page versus borrowed trust.
**Apply when.** Ad spend goes up but revenue barely moves, and the conversion rate "drops" even though you fixed nothing.
**The move.** Treat any baseline built on warm traffic as fiction. When you scale budget into cold traffic and the rate collapses, that gap is the page's real ceiling — fix the obvious page defects first, then judge media.
**Evidence.** A founder tripled ad spend; revenue rose only 11%. Warm-traffic conversion of ~3% cratered to 0.5% on cold; fixing the obvious lifted it to 2% — 4× the real number.
**Source.** [@richardrx · 2026-06-08](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2063972594223661127)

## Write the CTA microcopy, not the button color
**Principle.** The CTA label moves conversion far more than button color — it's the last thing the user reads before deciding.
**Apply when.** A/B testing button colors while the label still says "Sign up" with no click trigger.
**The move.** Make the CTA answer three questions: (1) what happens when I click, (2) how long it takes, (3) what it costs/commits. "Sign up" answers none; "Start free in 30s" answers two. Add a click trigger: "Start Free Trial" + "14 days, no card". Rooted in outcome bias and Construal Level Theory.
**Source.** [@richardrx · 2026-05-25](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2058875777739866490)

## Cut CAC by filtering on the landing page, not the media
**Principle.** High CAC is rarely a media problem — it's usually a filtering problem. The landing page can qualify leads before the form.
**Apply when.** Clicks become leads, leads become calls, calls don't close, and founders blame the channel.
**The move.** Pull three filtering levers: (1) copy specificity — segment-specific language; (2) visible pricing — screens out no-budget leads; (3) a qualification question as the first interaction. CAC = cost per closed deal, not cost per lead.
**Source.** [@richardrx · 2026-05-18](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2056385204785213446)

## Aim for 4.2–4.5 stars, not a perfect 5
**Principle.** A flawless rating reads as fake; consumers distrust unanimity. Imperfection converts better than pure praise.
**Apply when.** Building or curating social proof on a landing page or product.
**The move.** Let imperfection show. Stack highest-converting combination: detailed case study with quantifiable ROI, ideally a video testimonial from a recognizable brand, plus client logos. Make each review verifiable.
**Evidence.** Northwestern: purchase probability peaks at 4.2–4.5 stars, not 5. Testimonials lift LP conversion up to 34% (VWO); 5+ reviews → 270% more likely to be bought (Yotpo).
**Source.** [@richardrx · 2026-05-14](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2054910531132183030)

## Compress the decision window
**Principle.** The purchase decision is mostly made before checkout. Conversion is architecture: the user converts when the path to action is shorter than the path to doubt.
**Apply when.** Optimizing the checkout, CTA, or headline while ignoring deliberation time upstream.
**The move.** Pressurize with real scarcity ("Only 1 room left at this price"), driven by loss aversion (Kahneman: losing hurts ~2× more than equivalent gain). Or remove steps entirely (Amazon 1-Click).
**Evidence.** Booking.com runs 1,000+ simultaneous experiments; showing sold-out hotels alongside available ones *raised* bookings by sharpening perceived scarcity.
**Source.** [@richardrx · 2026-05-11](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2053832356293738914)

## Don't clone the page — the converting layer is invisible
**Principle.** A converting sales page is the visible shell of an invisible system. Copying the layout copies what's cheapest to produce; the expensive part (customer research) stays invisible.
**Apply when.** A page "looks like it works" and you're tempted to clone it.
**The move.** Ask: what on this page is a function of the *product*, and what is a function of the *customer research*? The hidden layer: customer vocabulary from interviews, objections ordered to the ICP's specific fear, social proof hand-picked to resonate. This error has a name: cargo cult.
**Source.** [@richardrx — Design for startups]
